Narration lab

Hear the story

The device cannot speak with a neural voice at runtime — narration is pre-recorded clips in flash. So the clips are rendered offline from the closed 3,450-word table (Kokoro-82M, Apache-2.0, one fixed voice) and played back with the rule the firmware will use: a whole-line clip when the line is one the engine knows, otherwise the line assembled word by word. Everything you hear on this page is those clips; no server is involved.

1 · Play a story

Three stories the shipped model actually generated. Composed is the device's path: the 100 most-used lines play as whole-line clips, and every other line is spliced out of the phrases the templates are made of — each phrase cut out of a real recorded sentence, so it keeps sentence prosody and the way it runs on into the next word. Words only is the old fallback, kept here because it is the reason the composed units exist: one word at a time, every word is its own little sentence with its own falling pitch.

2 · What this costs on the device

Measured from the actual clips. The composed inventory is 2,744 units — 473 phrase takes (mean 2.56 s) and 2,271 slot words cut out of sentences (mean 0.33 s) — 32.6 min of audio, and it covers every line the templates can produce (all 4,000 sampled corpus lines reconstruct exactly, no word falls back). The old word library is 3,450 clips, 37.8 min; the 100 hot lines are 6.4 min. The 8 MB part leaves about 4.4 MB for voice (app ≈2.25 MB + INT4 model 1.05 MB + metadata ≈0.2 MB).

This page streams 1.5 MB of hot lines and 6.3 MB of composed units as CBR MP3 (the word library is a separate 7.4 MB button). The device would store the same clips as IMA ADPCM (no decoder) or Opus (needs a decoder). Cutting the words out of real sentences is both better and smaller: a slot word drops from 0.658 s to 0.33 s, and its terminal pitch slope from −270 Hz/s to −5 Hz/s, which is the difference between reading a list and carrying on with a sentence.

3 · Hear any word

Every word of the closed table has a clip. Hear it alone (a complete utterance, falling pitch at the end — this is what makes word-by-word narration sound like list reading) and as the mid-sentence take the composed path uses for slot words.

4 · One line, three ways

The same sentence as a whole-line clip, composed from its phrases, and assembled word by word. The first is the top tier (only the hot lines have one); the second is what the device does for every other line; the third is why it no longer does that.

5 · Pick a voice

Seven Kokoro voices, each saying three words and one line. The library shipped above is af_heart; re-rendering the whole vocabulary for another voice costs about 9 minutes of CPU.

6 · What the measurements showed

Pace. The same 240-word story takes 177 s word-by-word against 111 s at the reference narration pace; with the hot-line tier it lands at 118 s.

Every word is its own sentence. A bare word is synthesised as a complete utterance: 75 % of the 3,450 clips end on a pitch fall of more than 20 Hz.

Joins are clean. Clip onsets start from silence (sample step 0.00 against 0.038 inside clips), so concatenation clicks are not the problem — the rhythm is.

Loudness. Per-clip RMS spreads 1.49× (p90/p10), so per-clip gain is worth adding before packing.

Intelligibility (proxy). Whisper-tiny.en transcribed the word-by-word story at 11.2 % WER and the hot-line version at 6.2 % — the ASR's own floor inflates both, but the hybrid halves the errors.

These are synthetic takes. Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0) rendered offline; the plan is still to re-record with a human voice. The clips here are a distributable stand-in.
